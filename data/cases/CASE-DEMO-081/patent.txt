(54) Battery management system with adaptive cell balancing

(57) Abstract
A battery management system comprising a plurality of cell monitoring units (12) each measuring a voltage and a temperature of a battery cell; a balancing circuit (14) comprising a switchable bleed resistor for each cell; the invention reduces balancing losses by approximately 38 % and extends usable pack capacity by 4 % over 1,000 cycles.

Description
Technical field
The present invention relates to battery management systems for lithium-ion traction batteries, and in particular to a battery management system and a corresponding method.

Background art
A first known approach uses passive balancing with a fixed voltage threshold; however, it wastes energy at low temperature where cell voltages diverge transiently. A second known approach relies on fully active inductive balancing, which adds cost and electromagnetic interference to every cell. Neither approach provides reliable operation over the full range of operating conditions at acceptable cost.

Summary of the invention
It is an object of the invention to provide a battery management system overcoming these drawbacks. The object is achieved by a battery management system comprising a plurality of cell monitoring units (12) each measuring a voltage and a temperature of a battery cell; a balancing circuit (14) comprising a switchable bleed resistor for each cell; a controller (16) configured to estimate a state of charge of each cell from the measured voltage; wherein the controller activates the bleed resistor of a cell only when its state of charge exceeds the pack mean by more than an adaptive threshold that decreases with pack temperature. Compared with the prior art, the invention reduces balancing losses by approximately 38 % and extends usable pack capacity by 4 % over 1,000 cycles.

Detailed description of embodiments
In a 96-cell pack the controller (16) samples every cell at 10 Hz; at 25 °C the threshold is 2 %, falling linearly to 0.5 % at -10 °C. In a variant, the adaptive threshold is between 0.5 % and 3 % state of charge; in a further variant, the controller suspends balancing when any cell temperature exceeds 45 °C; in a further variant, the balancing circuit further comprises an inductive transfer stage (18) moving charge from the highest to the lowest cell. The features of the embodiments may be combined unless they are technically incompatible.

Claims
1. A battery management system comprising: a plurality of cell monitoring units (12) each measuring a voltage and a temperature of a battery cell; a balancing circuit (14) comprising a switchable bleed resistor for each cell; a controller (16) configured to estimate a state of charge of each cell from the measured voltage; wherein the controller activates the bleed resistor of a cell only when its state of charge exceeds the pack mean by more than an adaptive threshold that decreases with pack temperature.
2. The battery management system according to claim 1, wherein the adaptive threshold is between 0.5 % and 3 % state of charge.
3. The battery management system according to claim 1, wherein the controller suspends balancing when any cell temperature exceeds 45 °C.
4. The battery management system according to claim 1, wherein the balancing circuit further comprises an inductive transfer stage (18) moving charge from the highest to the lowest cell.
5. The battery management system according to claim 1, wherein the state of charge is estimated with an extended Kalman filter.
6. The battery management system according to claim 2 or 3, wherein the cell monitoring units communicate with the controller over an isolated daisy-chain bus (20).
7. A method of balancing cells of a traction battery, comprising: measuring a voltage and a temperature of each cell; estimating a state of charge of each cell; activating a bleed resistor of a cell whose state of charge exceeds the pack mean by more than an adaptive threshold that decreases with pack temperature.
8. The method according to claim 7, wherein the adaptive threshold is between 0.5 % and 3 % state of charge.
